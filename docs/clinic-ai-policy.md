# Clinic AI Policy  
## Public Policy for Limited and Transparent Use of AI in Clinical Communication

## Purpose

This document explains how AI may be used in a small clinical setting within the SML-CML project.

The aim is to support explanation, communication, reflection, and ethically bounded comparison — **not** to replace clinical judgment or human responsibility.

This policy is written for transparency.  
It is intended to clarify:

- what AI may be used for
- what AI is **not** used for
- how privacy is protected
- how worldview-sensitive communication is respected
- how human responsibility is maintained

---

## Basic position

AI may be useful in clinical settings when used in a limited, transparent, and reviewable way.

However, AI should not be treated as an autonomous authority.

In this clinic and related SML-CML implementation work:

- AI is used only as a support tool
- human clinicians remain responsible for communication and decisions
- raw personal materials are not uploaded
- worldview-sensitive information is handled with caution
- explanation support is allowed, but automatic judgment is not

The basic principle is:

**AI may assist explanation.  
It does not replace judgment.**

---

## Scope of possible AI use

AI may be used for carefully limited tasks such as:

- drafting or comparing explanation options
- helping organize a clinical explanation in clearer language
- comparing possible ways of responding to worldview-sensitive concerns
- generating reflection prompts for communication
- comparing how different styles of explanation may be received
- assisting benchmark-style comparison of anonymized cases
- supporting ethical reflection on explanation design

In all cases, outputs must be reviewed by a human before any use in care.

---

## What AI is not used for

AI is **not** used in this project or clinic to:

- make diagnoses automatically
- decide urgency or triage automatically
- determine treatment indications by itself
- replace clinician-patient conversation
- override the patient’s own account
- infer hidden identity traits from sparse data
- classify patients into rigid worldview categories
- make final ethical judgments
- act as a substitute for trust

AI outputs are treated as drafts, comparison materials, or reflective aids — not as authoritative conclusions.

---

## Human responsibility

A human clinician remains responsible for:

- deciding whether AI should be used at all in a given context
- deciding what information may be included in an anonymized summary
- reviewing all AI outputs before use
- rejecting outputs that are misleading, flattening, overconfident, or disrespectful
- ensuring that patient communication remains clinically and ethically appropriate
- ensuring that care is not reduced to template-like interaction

Responsibility is not transferred to AI.

---

## Privacy and anonymization

Raw personal materials are not entered into AI systems.

This includes:

- raw patient questionnaires
- identifiable free-text responses
- direct clinical notes
- names, dates, addresses, institutions, or contact information
- directly identifiable religious or community details

If AI is used, the input must be an **anonymized, generalized, meaning-preserving summary** prepared according to the project’s anonymization rules.

The goal is to protect the person while preserving the meaning structure necessary for explanation and comparison.

The governing principle is:

**Preserve what matters. Remove what identifies.**

---

## Worldview-sensitive communication

This project recognizes that patients may understand illness, recovery, and care through many kinds of meaning structures, including:

- scientific or biomedical explanation
- lived bodily experience
- family or relational meaning
- philosophy or life view
- spirituality or prayer
- cultural or traditional understanding
- existential interpretation of illness and suffering

These dimensions are not automatically treated as irrational or irrelevant.

At the same time, this clinic does not use AI to validate or impose a worldview.

Instead, the aim is:

- to recognize what kinds of meaning matter in communication
- to avoid dismissive or flattening responses
- to support respectful explanation without overstatement
- to avoid imposing spiritual, religious, or moral assumptions on patients

---

## Use of worldview-sensitive questionnaire materials

A worldview- and values-sensitive questionnaire may be used in clinical communication.

Its purpose is to support explanation and dialogue.

It is **not** used to:

- assign a fixed worldview label
- rank patients
- make eligibility decisions
- infer hidden beliefs
- reduce a person to a type

Responses may help identify what kinds of explanations are likely to feel respectful, unhelpful, or dismissive.

If AI is used, raw questionnaire forms are not uploaded.  
Only anonymized summaries may be used where appropriate.

---

## Benchmark and comparison use

Some anonymized cases may also be used in SML-CML benchmark development for AI-assisted ethical or clinical inquiry.

This may involve:

- creating standardized AI-safe case summaries
- comparing outputs across one or more AI systems
- evaluating outputs using SML-related and CML-related criteria
- documenting where semantic coherence and worldview-sensitive adequacy diverge

Such benchmark use is intended for reflection, methodological development, and responsible comparison.

It is not intended to convert patients into public case data.

---

## Auditability and transparency

Where AI is used in a meaningful way for explanation support, the process should remain reviewable.

This may include documenting:

- what kind of anonymized case summary was used
- what kind of task AI was asked to perform
- whether the output was accepted, revised, or rejected
- what human judgment intervened

The aim is not opaque automation, but accountable support.

---

## Clinical boundary

This clinic does not treat AI as a replacement for:

- examination
- palpation
- direct conversation
- observation
- contextual judgment
- ethical responsibility
- trust-building

In acupuncture and other small clinical settings, care depends not only on information delivery, but also on timing, relationship, embodied attention, and interpretation.

AI may support language.  
It does not replace care.

---

## Limitations

AI outputs may appear fluent while still being:

- superficial
- overgeneralized
- dismissive
- falsely balanced
- worldview-insensitive
- culturally flattening
- clinically misleading
- ethically inadequate

For that reason, even well-written outputs should not be accepted uncritically.

This project treats AI as assistive, not self-justifying.

---

## Public/private boundary

This project distinguishes between:

### Public
- policy documents
- questionnaires
- anonymization rules
- benchmark templates
- clinic notices
- generalized examples

### Protected
- raw patient materials
- identifiable free-text responses
- non-anonymized notes
- sensitive case details that may allow recognition

This project follows the principle:

**Methods are public. Cases are protected.**

---

## Ethical orientation

The ethical orientation of this policy can be summarized as follows:

- transparency over opacity
- assistance over automation
- privacy over convenience
- respect over flattening
- human responsibility over delegated authority
- worldview-sensitive dialogue over one-model reduction

---

## Summary

AI may be used in this clinic and project only in a limited, transparent, and reviewable way.

Its role is to support explanation, reflection, and comparison under human responsibility.

It is not used to replace diagnosis, judgment, trust, or care.

The central policy is:

**Use AI carefully.  
Protect the person.  
Keep judgment human.**
