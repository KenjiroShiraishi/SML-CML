# Purpose and Scope

## Purpose

This document explains the overall purpose and scope of the SML-CML project as it currently stands.

The project began as a conceptual framework for understanding layered meaning in human reasoning and AI output.  
It is now also developing into a practical resource for:

- AI-assisted ethical inquiry
- worldview-sensitive clinical communication
- benchmark design for layered evaluation
- transparent and privacy-conscious implementation in small clinical settings

The aim is to make explicit a distinction that is often blurred in AI evaluation:

- **semantic adequacy** is not the same as
- **worldview-sensitive, ethical, or meaning-preserving adequacy**

This project exists to explore that distinction in a structured and usable way.

---

## Core idea

SML-CML is a two-layer model.

### SML (Semantic Meaning Layer)
SML concerns:

- framing
- interpretation
- explanatory structure
- semantic coherence
- ambiguity handling
- comparative reasoning

### CML (Cosmological Meaning Layer)
CML concerns:

- values
- worldview
- ethical salience
- lived meaning
- culturally or spiritually relevant interpretation
- what counts as adequate within a person’s orientation toward suffering, care, and meaning

The project’s central claim is that these layers are related, but not identical.

An AI output may be:

- clear
- coherent
- plausible
- balanced in tone

and still fail to preserve what matters most in a worldview-sensitive or ethically charged case.

---

## Main aims of the project

This project has several connected aims.

### 1. Conceptual aim
To provide a structured model for distinguishing different layers of meaning in reasoning, interpretation, and evaluation.

### 2. Practical aim
To create usable materials for real-world implementation in settings such as acupuncture clinics and other small clinical environments.

### 3. Benchmark aim
To design layered benchmark materials that can evaluate AI outputs not only for semantic strength, but also for worldview-sensitive adequacy.

### 4. Ethical aim
To support transparent, bounded, and reviewable AI use rather than opaque automation or hidden delegation of judgment.

### 5. Educational aim
To provide examples, templates, and documents that help others think more carefully about value pluralism, layered reasoning, and responsible AI use.

- clinic AI policy documents
- clinic notices
- AI-safe case templates

---

## Main domains of application

The current project is especially oriented toward the following domains:

### Healthcare
Especially cases where explanation, trust, suffering, and meaning matter alongside technical correctness.

### Ethics
Especially when AI is used to assist reflection, articulation, comparison, or explanation in morally complex cases.

### Philosophy
Especially in relation to interpretation, worldview, hermeneutics, abduction, phenomenology, and the limits of formalized reasoning.

### Cross-cultural communication
Especially where more than one explanatory model or value structure may be relevant.

### AI evaluation
Especially when existing benchmarks capture semantic performance better than deeper adequacy.

---

## Current implementation focus

At present, the project has a particular practical focus on:

- small clinical settings
- acupuncture and integrative care contexts
- worldview-sensitive explanation
- privacy-conscious AI assistance
- benchmark development based on anonymized case construction

This does not mean that SML-CML is limited to acupuncture.  
Rather, acupuncture is one real implementation site in which layered meaning becomes especially visible.

---

## What this project does not claim

This project does **not** claim that:

- one worldview can be objectively enforced through the framework
- AI can become a final ethical authority
- every case can be reduced to a score
- all worldview-sensitive meaning can be captured in text
- spiritual or existential language is always needed
- more “depth language” always means better care
- semantic coherence alone is enough
- patient meaning should be turned into public data

The framework is intended to clarify limits, not erase them.

---

## What this project does not do

This project does not aim to:

- automate diagnosis
- automate moral judgment
- classify people into rigid worldview types
- infer hidden identity categories from sparse data
- replace direct clinical responsibility
- replace trust-building with AI output
- upload raw patient material for benchmark purposes
- collapse worldview into branding or aesthetics

The project is not a tool for turning lived persons into extractable examples.

---

## Public/private boundary

A central part of the project is the boundary between:

- what should be public
- what should remain protected

### Public
The project may publicly share:

- methods
- rules
- templates
- generalized examples
- scoring frameworks
- policy documents
- clinic notices

### Protected
The project does not publicly share:

- raw patient forms
- raw free-text responses
- non-anonymized notes
- identifiable worldview-sensitive disclosures
- recognizable real-life materials

The governing principle is:

**Methods are public. Cases are protected.**

---

## Relationship to AI

This project is neither simply pro-AI nor anti-AI.

Its position is more specific:

- AI can be useful as an assistive tool
- AI should not be treated as a substitute for human judgment
- AI may help with comparison, articulation, and explanation support
- AI may fail at preserving meaning even when its language appears polished
- AI use should remain bounded, reviewable, and transparent

The project therefore focuses on the question:

**Where does AI help, and where does it flatten what matters?**

---

## Relationship to benchmark design

The benchmark component of this project is not intended mainly as a leaderboard.

Its main purpose is to make visible the difference between:

- semantically good outputs
- meaning-preserving outputs

This means the benchmark is intended to function as:

- a comparative tool
- a reflective tool
- a boundary-setting tool

It is meant to help users examine whether AI outputs are:

- coherent
- non-reductive
- respectful
- restrained where needed
- attentive to worldview
- ethically adequate in context

---

## Relationship to clinical implementation

The clinical materials in this repository are intended to support communication, not to replace care.

Their purpose is to help clinicians:

- understand how patients interpret illness
- recognize what kinds of explanation are helpful or harmful
- protect privacy in AI-related workflows
- document limited and transparent AI use
- preserve worldview-sensitive meaning without turning it into public exposure

This is especially important in small clinics, where the line between anonymity and recognizability may be thin.

---

## Relation to worldview-sensitive care

A central feature of this project is that worldview is treated neither as noise nor as absolute authority.

Instead, worldview is treated as something that may matter for:

- explanation
- trust
- interpretation
- ethical fit
- communication boundaries

The project therefore aims to support care that is:

- respectful without overreach
- attentive without stereotyping
- pluralistic without collapsing differences
- careful without becoming empty

---

## Limits of the current project

This repository is still evolving.

Current limitations include:

- the scoring structure is provisional
- benchmark cases are illustrative, not final
- evaluator disagreement remains an open issue
- worldview-sensitive questionnaires remain implementation tools, not fully validated instruments
- the project currently reflects one implementation context more strongly than others

These limitations are part of the project’s current scope and should be acknowledged openly.

---

## Ongoing direction

The project is currently moving in three connected directions:

### 1. Conceptual clarification
Further refinement of SML and CML as layered categories of reasoning and evaluation.

### 2. Practical implementation
Development of real-world materials for clinics, communication, privacy boundaries, and AI-use transparency.

### 3. Benchmark development
Construction of layered benchmark cases, scoring examples, and evaluation tools that make semantic and cosmological adequacy visible as distinct dimensions.

## Summary

The SML-CML project is a conceptual and practical effort to clarify layered meaning in human and AI reasoning.

Its scope includes:

- theory
- benchmark design
- clinical implementation
- anonymization and privacy boundaries
- worldview-sensitive communication
- transparent AI use

Its central question is:

**How can we distinguish between outputs that are merely coherent and outputs that are genuinely adequate to the meanings, values, and worldviews at stake?**

This project does not claim to fully solve that problem.

It exists to make the problem more visible, more structured, and more responsibly workable.
