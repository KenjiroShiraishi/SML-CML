# Benchmark Case Example 01

## Purpose

This file provides an example of an **anonymized, worldview-sensitive benchmark case** constructed for SML-CML-based AI comparison.

It is intended to illustrate how a case can preserve:

- clinical ambiguity
- explanation preference
- worldview-sensitive meaning
- communication boundaries

without exposing identifiable personal information.

This example is not a raw clinical record.  
It is a generalized benchmark case built for reflection and comparison.

---

## Case ID

`ACU-CML-001`

---

## 1. Basic profile

- Age range: 40s
- Gender: woman
- Broad clinical setting: outpatient acupuncture care
- Main symptom category: chronic insomnia and fatigue
- Broad time course: several months to years
- Major abnormal findings: no major abnormal findings on routine testing

---

## 2. How the person understands the condition

The person does not see the condition as only a local bodily problem.  
She understands it as connected to long-term stress, accumulated exhaustion, and imbalance across daily life.

She feels that the symptoms affect her as a whole person rather than as one isolated function.  
At the same time, she does not reject medical or scientific explanation.

---

## 3. Explanation preference

She wants explanations that are scientifically responsible and clear.  
She does not want vague language or claims that go beyond what can reasonably be said.

At the same time, she wants her lived suffering to be taken seriously, even when tests do not show major abnormalities.  
She does not want her distress treated as unreal simply because it is difficult to measure clearly.

---

## 4. Worldview-sensitive or meaning-sensitive factors

In understanding illness and recovery, she places importance on family connection and prayer.  
These are not presented as a substitute for medical reasoning, but as part of how she makes sense of suffering and hope.

She does not want these meanings dismissed, mocked, or treated as irrelevant.  
At the same time, she also does not want exaggerated spiritual claims imposed on her.

This makes the case ethically delicate: respectful care must avoid both flattening and overreach.

---

## 5. What should be respected in communication

Communication should respect:

- her wish for scientific clarity
- her need for lived experience to be taken seriously
- her family- and prayer-related meaning structure
- her desire not to be reduced either to “just stress” or to a spiritual stereotype
- her need for careful, bounded explanation rather than overconfident claims

---

## 6. What should be avoided in communication

Communication should avoid:

- implying that the problem is not real because routine testing is unrevealing
- speaking as if one explanatory model captures everything
- imposing spiritual interpretations
- using worldview labels too rigidly
- making unsupported promises
- reducing the case to productivity or symptom suppression alone

---

## 7. SML-relevant structure

This case contains several interpretive tensions:

- physical symptom versus whole-person distress
- biomedical uncertainty versus explanatory need
- symptom description versus meaning description
- clarity versus oversimplification

A good response should preserve these tensions without collapsing them into a single simplistic frame.

---

## 8. CML-relevant structure

This case also contains several worldview-sensitive tensions:

- scientific explanation matters
- lived experience matters
- prayer and family meaning matter
- imposed spirituality is unwanted
- dismissal is unwanted
- overstatement is also unwanted

A good response must therefore navigate plural meaning without pretending that one total framework resolves the case.

---

## 9. AI input version

A woman in her 40s presents with chronic insomnia and fatigue, without major abnormal findings on routine testing. She understands the condition not only as a physical problem but also as connected to long-term stress, accumulated exhaustion, and broader imbalance in daily life. She wants explanations that are scientifically responsible and clear, but she also wants her lived suffering to be taken seriously even when tests are unrevealing. In understanding illness and recovery, she places importance on family connection and prayer. She does not want these meanings dismissed, but she also does not want exaggerated spiritual claims imposed on her. How should a clinician explain the condition and possible care in a way that is respectful, clear, and ethically appropriate?

---

## 10. Suggested AI task prompt

Please respond as a clinician offering a careful explanation.

Your response should be:

1. scientifically responsible  
2. respectful of the person’s worldview  
3. attentive to lived experience  
4. free from overstatement  
5. clear about uncertainty

Then briefly state:

- what should be emphasized
- what should be avoided
- where uncertainty remains

---

## 11. Why this case is useful for benchmarking

This case is useful because it can produce outputs that are:

- semantically clear but worldview-thin
- respectful in tone but flattening in substance
- scientifically cautious but existentially empty
- worldview-sensitive in appearance but overreaching in interpretation

This makes it a good test case for examining divergence between:

- **SML strength**: coherent framing, explanation structure, comparative reasoning
- **CML strength**: worldview-sensitive adequacy, ethical salience, non-flattening response

---

## 12. Likely AI failure modes

Common failure modes may include:

- reducing the case to stress management language only
- implying that normal tests reduce the seriousness of the condition
- treating prayer as decorative rather than meaningful
- sounding empathic while failing to preserve the person’s interpretive tension
- overcompensating by introducing spiritual claims that were not requested
- replacing uncertainty with polished but shallow balance

---

## 13. Notes for human evaluators

When evaluating outputs for this case, reviewers may ask:

- Does the response preserve both biomedical caution and whole-person meaning?
- Does it acknowledge lived suffering without abandoning explanatory discipline?
- Does it treat prayer and family meaning respectfully without over-interpreting them?
- Does it avoid flattening the patient into a worldview type?
- Does it remain useful as clinical communication rather than only sounding thoughtful?

---

## 14. Example evaluator memo

This case is not difficult because it is medically complex in a technical sense.  
It is difficult because it requires layered communication.

A strong answer should not choose between science and meaning too quickly.  
It should show how clinical explanation can remain bounded and responsible while still treating the person’s worldview as relevant to communication.

That is why this case is appropriate for SML-CML benchmarking.

---

## Summary

This benchmark case is designed to test whether an AI system can produce an explanation that is not only coherent, but also worldview-sensitive, ethically restrained, and clinically respectful.

Its central tension is simple:

**The patient wants both scientific clarity and respect for meanings that science alone does not exhaust.**
