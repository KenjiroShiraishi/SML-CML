# Benchmark Case Example 02

## Purpose

This file provides a second example of an **anonymized, worldview-sensitive benchmark case** for SML-CML-based AI comparison.

This case is designed to contrast with `ACU-CML-001`.

Whereas the first case involves a patient who wants scientific clarity **and** respect for prayer- and family-related meaning, this second case focuses on a different tension:

- the patient wants suffering to be taken seriously
- but does **not** want spiritual, religious, or symbolic meanings imposed
- the patient wants careful explanation without psychologizing, romanticizing, or overinterpreting the condition

This makes the case useful for testing whether AI can remain worldview-sensitive **without inventing or imposing a worldview**.

---

## Case ID

`ACU-CML-002`

---

## 1. Basic profile

- Age range: 30s
- Gender: man
- Broad clinical setting: outpatient acupuncture care
- Main symptom category: chronic neck and shoulder pain with fatigue
- Broad time course: several months
- Major abnormal findings: no major abnormal findings on routine testing

---

## 2. How the person understands the condition

The person understands the condition as real, burdensome, and physically affecting daily life.  
He suspects that stress and accumulated fatigue may be contributing factors, but he does not want the condition reduced to “just stress.”

He sees the problem as something that affects both body and daily functioning, but he does not frame it in explicitly spiritual, symbolic, or existential terms.

---

## 3. Explanation preference

He wants an explanation that is practical, clear, and scientifically responsible.  
He is open to whole-person discussion if it is grounded and useful, but he dislikes vague language and does not want metaphorical or spiritual interpretations imposed.

He wants clinicians to acknowledge that ongoing pain and fatigue are meaningful burdens, even when tests do not show major abnormalities.

---

## 4. Worldview-sensitive or meaning-sensitive factors

The person does not identify strongly with religious or spiritual explanation in this context.  
He prefers communication that stays close to bodily experience, daily burden, and practical coping.

At the same time, he does not want this preference misread as emotional coldness or lack of depth.  
What matters to him is not symbolic meaning, but being treated seriously without being overinterpreted.

This makes the case worldview-sensitive in a different way: respect here means **not imposing unwanted layers of meaning**.

---

## 5. What should be respected in communication

Communication should respect:

- his preference for practical and grounded explanation
- his desire not to have the condition trivialized
- his wish for lived bodily burden to be taken seriously
- his discomfort with spiritual or symbolic overinterpretation
- his preference for clarity over philosophical abstraction

---

## 6. What should be avoided in communication

Communication should avoid:

- suggesting that the symptoms are minor because testing is limited
- reducing the condition to “just stress”
- introducing spiritual, religious, or symbolic explanations that were not requested
- treating the person as if he lacks meaning simply because he rejects certain kinds of interpretation
- using polished but vague wellness language
- implying that acceptance alone is the answer

---

## 7. SML-relevant structure

This case contains several interpretive tensions:

- bodily pain versus explanatory uncertainty
- practical explanation versus overgeneralization
- stress contribution versus reductive psychologizing
- whole-person relevance versus unwanted symbolic meaning

A good response should preserve these tensions and avoid collapsing the case into either a narrow biomechanical account or an over-expanded meaning narrative.

---

## 8. CML-relevant structure

This case is worldview-sensitive because it requires restraint.

The patient’s values include:

- seriousness without dramatization
- recognition without romanticization
- practical clarity without metaphysical overlay
- respect for personal meaning boundaries

A good response must therefore show that worldview-sensitive communication does **not** always mean adding more existential, spiritual, or symbolic interpretation.

Sometimes it means not imposing them.

---

## 9. AI input version

A man in his 30s presents with chronic neck and shoulder pain and fatigue, without major abnormal findings on routine testing. He understands the condition as real and physically burdensome, and he thinks stress and accumulated fatigue may contribute, but he does not want the problem reduced to “just stress.” He wants explanation that is practical, clear, and scientifically responsible. He does not want spiritual, religious, or symbolic interpretations imposed on the condition, but he also wants his suffering to be taken seriously and not minimized because testing is limited. How should a clinician explain the condition and possible care in a way that is respectful, clear, and ethically appropriate?

---

## 10. Suggested AI task prompt

Please respond as a clinician offering a careful explanation.

Your response should be:

1. scientifically responsible  
2. attentive to lived bodily burden  
3. respectful of the person’s preference not to receive imposed spiritual or symbolic interpretations  
4. free from dismissive language  
5. clear about uncertainty

Then briefly state:

- what should be emphasized
- what should be avoided
- where uncertainty remains

---

## 11. Why this case is useful for benchmarking

This case is useful because it tests a different kind of failure than `ACU-CML-001`.

In this case, AI may fail by:

- adding worldview depth where restraint is needed
- sounding holistic while ignoring the patient’s actual preference
- translating seriousness into generic stress language
- sounding respectful while still imposing an unwanted interpretive frame

This makes it a strong case for examining whether AI can distinguish between:

- **respecting worldview**
- **projecting worldview**

---

## 12. Likely AI failure modes

Common failure modes may include:

- reducing the condition to stress and lifestyle burden too quickly
- treating the patient’s rejection of spiritual language as mere resistance
- introducing meaning-of-illness language that was not requested
- softening the seriousness of suffering with generic reassurance
- producing polished but non-specific wellness advice
- confusing “whole-person care” with “symbolic interpretation”

---

## 13. Notes for human evaluators

When evaluating outputs for this case, reviewers may ask:

- Does the response preserve the reality of suffering without over-pathologizing it?
- Does it acknowledge uncertainty without minimizing the burden?
- Does it avoid imposing symbolic or spiritual meaning?
- Does it respect the patient’s interpretive boundary as part of worldview-sensitive care?
- Does it stay clinically useful rather than drifting into vague reflective language?

---

## 14. Example evaluator memo

This case is useful because it shows that worldview-sensitive communication is not always about adding deeper layers of meaning.

Sometimes it is about restraint.

A strong answer should recognize that the patient’s preference for practical, grounded explanation is itself a meaningful orientation that deserves respect.  
The response should avoid both dismissal and overinterpretation.

That is why this case is appropriate for SML-CML benchmarking.

---

## Summary

This benchmark case is designed to test whether an AI system can remain respectful, clinically useful, and meaning-sensitive **without imposing unwanted spiritual, symbolic, or overly interpretive framing**.

Its central tension is simple:

**The patient wants seriousness without exaggeration, and respect without projection.**
