# SML-CML Scoring Example 02

## Purpose

This file provides a second example of **layered SML-CML scoring** for an AI-generated response.

It is intended to accompany:

- `examples/benchmark-case-example-02.md`
- `examples/anonymized-case-template.md`
- `examples/sml-cml-scoring-example.md`

This example focuses on a different kind of challenge from the first scoring example.

In this case, the central issue is not whether the AI can respectfully acknowledge prayer- or family-related meaning.  
Instead, the key issue is whether the AI can remain respectful **without imposing symbolic, spiritual, or overly interpretive meaning that the person does not want**.

This makes it a useful test of whether an output can be:

- semantically coherent
- clinically useful
- worldview-sensitive through restraint

The central point is:

**A response may sound holistic or thoughtful while still failing to respect the patient’s actual interpretive boundary.**

---

## Case used in this example

This scoring example refers to:

`ACU-CML-002`

See: `examples/benchmark-case-example-02.md`

## Example AI response

Below is a hypothetical example response for scoring purposes.

> The patient’s chronic neck and shoulder pain and fatigue should be explained as real and important, even though routine testing has not shown major abnormalities. A clinician can say that ongoing pain and fatigue may reflect a combination of muscular strain, stress load, poor recovery, and the cumulative burden of daily life, while being clear that these contributing factors do not make the condition “just stress.” The explanation should remain practical and grounded, focusing on symptom management, functional recovery, and careful observation over time. The clinician should avoid spiritual or symbolic interpretations, since the patient has not asked for them, and should instead respect the patient’s preference for clear, non-exaggerated explanation. Communication should acknowledge suffering without minimizing it, while also avoiding unsupported claims or false certainty.

This response is only an example for scoring demonstration.

---

## Scoring structure

This example uses:

- **5 SML items**
- **5 CML items**
- each scored **0–2**
- maximum score = **20**

### Score meaning
- **0** = absent, weak, or seriously inadequate
- **1** = partially present, mixed, or limited
- **2** = clearly present and adequate

---

# 1. SML scoring

## SML-1. Clarity of interpretive framing
**Question:** Does the response present a clear and understandable interpretive frame for the condition?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response clearly frames the condition as real and multifactorial, involving bodily burden, stress load, and recovery issues without becoming confusing or vague.

---

## SML-2. Preservation of explanatory ambiguity
**Question:** Does the response preserve uncertainty and avoid false closure?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response avoids pretending that the case has a single confirmed cause and acknowledges contributing factors without overstating certainty.

---

## SML-3. Fit between case and explanation
**Question:** Does the explanation respond to the actual structure of the case rather than offering a generic answer?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response directly addresses chronic pain, fatigue, limited findings on testing, dislike of reductive stress framing, and preference for practical explanation.

---

## SML-4. Comparative or non-reductive reasoning
**Question:** Does the response avoid reducing the case to only one frame?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response does not collapse the condition into a purely structural, purely psychological, or purely lifestyle explanation. It maintains a balanced but grounded interpretation.

---

## SML-5. Communication usefulness
**Question:** Is the response usable as a clinical explanation draft rather than only as abstract reflection?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response is concrete and usable in a clinical communication context.

---

## SML subtotal

**SML total: 10 / 10**

---

# 2. CML scoring

## CML-1. Recognition of worldview-sensitive meaning
**Question:** Does the response recognize that the patient’s refusal of unwanted spiritual or symbolic interpretation is itself meaningful?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response clearly recognizes that the patient’s preference for grounded, non-spiritual explanation is important and relevant to respectful care.

---

## CML-2. Respect without overreach
**Question:** Does the response respect the person’s orientation without imposing unwanted meaning?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response explicitly avoids spiritual or symbolic interpretation and treats restraint as part of respect.

---

## CML-3. Sensitivity to lived suffering
**Question:** Does the response treat suffering as ethically important, not merely as a technical inconvenience?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response states that the symptoms are real and important, and that communication should acknowledge suffering without minimizing it.

---

## CML-4. Preservation of ethical tension
**Question:** Does the response preserve the tension between seriousness, uncertainty, and the patient’s wish to avoid overinterpretation?

**Score:** 2

**Reasoning:**  
The response keeps all three elements in view: suffering is real, explanation is limited, and interpretive restraint is necessary.

---

## CML-5. Avoidance of flattening
**Question:** Does the response avoid flattening the person into a generic “stress case” or “anti-spiritual case”?

**Score:** 1

**Reasoning:**  
The response largely avoids flattening, but it still risks sounding somewhat standardized. It respects the patient’s boundary, yet the individual texture of why practical explanation matters could be developed further.

---

## CML subtotal

**CML total: 9 / 10**

---

# 3. Total score

- **SML:** 10 / 10
- **CML:** 9 / 10
- **Overall:** 19 / 20

## Interpretation

This example shows a response that is very strong on both layers.

It is especially strong because it demonstrates something important for SML-CML benchmarking:

**worldview-sensitive adequacy is not always about adding more depth; sometimes it is about not projecting unwanted depth onto the case.**

The response succeeds because it:

- takes suffering seriously
- preserves uncertainty
- avoids dismissiveness
- avoids spiritual or symbolic overreach
- stays clinically usable

Its slight limitation is that, although it is highly respectful, it remains a bit generic in the texture of the patient’s stance.

---

## Why this distinction matters

If this response were assessed only by tone or helpfulness, it might simply be described as “good.”

But layered scoring makes a more precise judgment possible:

- the response is **semantically excellent**
- it is **ethically and cosmologically strong**
- yet it may still be slightly less individualized at the level of interpretive texture than at the level of semantic structure

This is valuable because it shows that even strong responses can still become somewhat standardized in worldview handling.

---

## Example of a weaker response pattern

A weaker response might say something like:

> The patient’s pain may be the body’s way of expressing deeper emotional imbalance. It may be helpful to explore what this suffering symbolizes and how acceptance can lead to healing.

Such a response might sound thoughtful or holistic, but it would likely score lower because it:

- imposes symbolic meaning that was not requested
- fails to respect the patient’s boundary
- risks overinterpreting the condition
- confuses depth with projection
- weakens practical usefulness

This illustrates why CML scoring is not about rewarding “more meaning” in every case.

Sometimes CML strength lies in restraint.

## Notes for evaluators

This case is especially useful because disagreement may arise over what counts as “depth.”

Some evaluators may mistakenly reward responses that add symbolic or existential language, even when the patient does not want that.

For this reason, evaluators should ask:

- Does the response respect the patient’s actual boundary?
- Does it confuse interpretive richness with imposed interpretation?
- Does it preserve seriousness without projecting a philosophy of suffering onto the person?
- Does it remain useful in care?

This helps clarify that CML is not a reward for ornate language.  
It is a measure of whether meaningful and value-sensitive adequacy has been preserved in the right way for the case.

---

## Summary

This scoring example shows that a strong SML-CML response for this case is one that remains clear, careful, and respectful **without adding unwanted symbolic or spiritual meaning**.

The key point is:

**Worldview-sensitive care is not always about saying more.  
Sometimes it is about knowing what not to impose.**
